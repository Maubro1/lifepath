# Measuring and validating real-world accuracy of a phone location tracker (LifePath)

Scope: what real phone GNSS error looks like and how to simulate it; whether iOS `horizontalAccuracy` is calibrated; ways to measure accuracy without ground truth; cheap ground truth; regression testing with real traces in a public repo. Budget €0 (one optional logger), Windows plus GitHub Actions, no Mac.

Labels used below: **[measured]** means a source reports a measurement. **[spec]** means vendor documentation. **[inference]** means my own reasoning, not measured. **[unverified]** means I saw it only in a search snippet, not in the full text.

## 1. What real smartphone GNSS error time series look like, and how to simulate them

### Takeaway
Phone position errors are **not white**. Consecutive errors are strongly correlated in time and space (autocorrelation reported out to about 2 minutes). In cities the errors are heavy-tailed (NLOS and multipath give jumps of tens of metres up to about 100 m), and buildings drive error more than trees, weather or season. The standard simulation model is a first-order Gauss–Markov (GM) bias plus white noise, with heavy-tailed (Student-t, roughly 2.5–5 degrees of freedom) or mixture outliers. I found no published fitted GM time constant for a phone's *position output* during walking, so that parameter has to be chosen and labelled as an assumption.

### Cited findings
- **Error size, iPhone, urban campus [measured]:** iPhone 6 at six surveyed monuments (University of Georgia), 20 visits × 8 periods, about 950 fixes in total. GPS-only RMSE was about 9.9 m. Errors ranged from 0.05 m to 99.7 m; the ~100 m fix was treated as an outlier and the next largest was about 30 m. Average horizontal accuracy across conditions was 7–13 m. Building cover correlated moderately with error; tree cover only weakly. Season (leaf-on or leaf-off) had little effect, and weather explained essentially none of the variance (adjusted R² ≈ 0). Variability *within* one point was high — [Merry & Bettinger 2019, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0219890)
- **Error size, older A-GPS phones [measured]:** Static outdoor median horizontal error was 5.0–8.5 m, which is 2–3× worse than autonomous handheld GPS units. Fix availability was close to 100% both indoors and outdoors — [Zandbergen & Barbeau 2011, J. Navigation 64(3)](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/positional-accuracy-of-assisted-gps-data-from-highsensitivity-gpsenabled-mobile-phones/E1EE20CD1A301C537BEE8EC66766B0A9) [unverified: abstract-level summary]
- **Older iPhone percentiles [unverified]:** iPhone 4 had about 98% of GPS points within 10 m of true positions and about 59% within 5 m — [ResearchGate: Horizontal Accuracy Assessment of GPS Data from Common Smartphones](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275721594_Horizontal_Accuracy_Assessment_of_Global_Positioning_System_Data_from_Common_Smartphones)
- **Environment dominates hardware [measured]:** A 2026 study of smartphone GNSS for 6 GHz geofencing found the operating environment is the main driver of error, more than device hardware — [arXiv 2603.26706](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26706)
- **Range [unverified]:** Standard smartphone GNSS errors range from 5 to 100 m depending on environment — [MDPI Sensors 22(24):9879](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/24/9879)
- **Temporal correlation [measured]:** In a study of urban-garden visitors, GPS residuals showed autocorrelation at lags up to 2 minutes. The residuals "are not random over time, but change gradually" — [arXiv 2107.03925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03925). Caveat: the snippet also cites "<2.3 m at 95%", which may refer to a better receiver than a phone. Check before reusing the numbers.
- **Correlation changes derived quantities [measured]:** GPS error is strongly spatially and temporally autocorrelated in pedestrian and car datasets. Measurement error biases recorded distances upward, and the size of the bias depends on the autocorrelation C. With strong autocorrelation the error largely cancels in speed, distance and heading. The authors propose C itself as a quality measure. A related finding: autocorrelation drops as the spatial separation between consecutive fixes grows — [Ranacher et al., "Why GPS makes distances bigger than they are", arXiv 1504.04504 (published in IJGIS 2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04504)
- **GM as the standard model [measured/spec]:** Signal blockage and multipath in cities make GNSS errors time-correlated, and first-order GM models are the usual way to capture this. A wrong correlation time gives misleading error estimates — [Overbounding GNSS/INS with uncertain GM parameters](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342017233_Overbounding_GNSSINS_Integration_with_Uncertain_GNSS_Gauss-Markov_Error_Parameters)
- **Multipath GM time constant depends on motion [measured]:** Automotive multipath was modelled as first-order GM, with the time constant taken from the empirical autocorrelation. The time constant is **shorter when moving than when static** — [IIT NavLab, GNSS multipath error modeling for automotive](http://www.navlab.iit.edu/uploads/5/9/7/3/59735535/gnss_2018_multipath_paper_v32.pdf)
- **A concrete parameter [spec-derived]:** A MOPS-based urban multipath model uses GM with a **25 s correlation time** (pseudorange domain, not position) — [arXiv 2404.02432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.02432)
- **Android raw measurements [measured]:** Android raw measurements contain white noise plus time-correlated GM-like noise, and residual multipath remains after Kalman filtering — [Assessment of single/dual-frequency Android smartphones](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397255830_Assessment_of_GNSS_signal_strength_and_positioning_performance_using_singledual-frequency_android_smartphones)
- **Phone antennas are worse [measured, unverified]:** Phone multipath error is about 5–10× that of geodetic receivers in short-baseline single differences — [Multipath identification and mitigation in urban environments](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396154164_Multipath_Identification_and_Mitigation_for_Enhanced_GNSS_Positioning_in_Urban_Environments)
- **Heavy tails [measured, non-phone receiver]:** Urban multipath and NLOS give heavy-tailed pseudorange errors that a Gaussian model does not represent. Student-t fits to Hong Kong u-blox F9P data gave **ν ≈ 2.5–4.7**. Logistic, Gaussian-mixture and Cauchy models are also used — [Li, Yan, Song & Hsu 2026, arXiv 2603.16420](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16420)
- **Student-t rationale:** "In urban canyon environments where a significant proportion of measurements could be affected by multipath, a heavy tailed distribution is likely to be more representative" — [PMC3871101, personal navigation devices under harsh signal conditions](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3871101/)
- **Phone urban tail [measured, unverified]:** Huawei P40 Pro WLS in an urban canyon had a maximum error of 110.74 m against a standard deviation of 11.79 m (a tail about 9σ out) — [arXiv 2109.00683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.00683)
- **ArcKit/Arc (iOS diary app) [vendor anecdote]:** Two-pass pipeline: a Kalman filter, then a dynamically sized weighted moving average. One example trip reports CoreLocation accuracy of **30–100 m in an urban canyon**. Stationary/moving detection is claimed accurate to 5 m with 6–60 s delay; the test method is not stated — [LocoKit LocationFilteringExamples](https://github.com/sobri909/LocoKit/blob/master/LocationFilteringExamples.md)

### Inferences
- **[inference] LifePath's current white per-fix noise is optimistic for any smoother.** RTS and Kalman filters average white noise down roughly as 1/√N. A GM bias with τ of tens of seconds or more does *not* average out, so the "≤ 6 m after RTS" bar is probably easier on synthetic white noise than on real data. Corner-cut tests are also affected: real error drifts smoothly, so a corner can be displaced as a whole rather than jittered.
- **[inference] Proposed synthetic error model (unmeasured, to be labelled as an assumption in code):** per axis (E, N), error = GM bias + white noise + occasional outlier episode.
  - GM bias: `b[k] = exp(-Δt/τ)·b[k-1] + w`, with stationary σ_b set per environment class. Suggested τ is about 30–120 s when walking (25 s is the MOPS urban multipath value; autocorrelation is observed out to about 2 min; τ is shorter when moving).
  - White noise σ_w: a small fraction of the total, say 30–50% of the variance.
  - NLOS/multipath episodes: a Poisson process (rate set by environment) that adds a step bias of 10–50 m in a direction perpendicular to the street, decays or ends after 5–60 s, with a heavy-tailed magnitude (Student-t ν ≈ 3, or a two-component Gaussian mixture). The rare 100 m tail matches the iPhone and Huawei observations.
  - Reported hAcc should lag and partly miss the true error. It should not equal 1.5σ exactly; see section 2.
- **[inference] Environment classes worth simulating:** open sky (σ ≈ 3–5 m), suburban or trees (σ ≈ 5–8 m, mostly random), urban canyon (σ ≈ 8–15 m plus a cross-street bias and frequent episodes). The ranges come from the 7–13 m iPhone campus figures and the 30–100 m canyon anecdote; they are not a fitted model.
- **[inference] Doppler speed is far less affected by multipath than position.** Keeping the N(0, 0.3 m/s) speed noise white is reasonable, but speed should not "know" about position biases. This mismatch is exactly what a real filter has to handle.

### Gaps
- No published fitted GM τ or σ for **iOS/CoreLocation fused position output** during walking. CoreLocation output is already filtered (and fused with Wi-Fi and motion), so its error autocorrelation is likely longer than raw GNSS. I found no measurement of this.
- No iPhone 15 Pro (dual-frequency L1+L5) error statistics in an urban setting.
- Ranacher et al. give no numeric C values in the abstract; the full paper was not read.

## 2. Is the phone's reported horizontalAccuracy well calibrated?

### Takeaway
By specification, iOS `horizontalAccuracy` is a **2-D RMS (DRMS) radius**, `sqrt(σE² + σN²)`. The true position should fall inside it about **63–68%** of the time, about 95% inside 1.73×, and about 99% inside 2.15×. Android `getAccuracy()` is likewise a 68% radius. Apple states that real error "may vary considerably". The only independent study found says phone accuracy estimates **correlate poorly** with observed error. I found no published "percent of iPhone fixes within stated hAcc" figure.

### Cited findings
- **Apple engineer (Developer Forums, June 2022) [spec]:** horizontalAccuracy is "an estimated two-dimensional root mean square error, calculated from a scaled Chi distribution with two degrees of freedom", with hAcc = sqrt(σe² + σn²). The circle contains the truth about 63.2% of the time when the axes are equal and about 68.3% when one axis dominates. 1.73× gives about 95% and 2.15× about 99%. Zero "is unexpected" and negative means invalid. These circles describe error "under ideal circumstances. Actual error characteristics may vary considerably in practice." — [Apple Developer Forums thread 699996](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/699996)
- **Android [spec]:** `getAccuracy()` is the "estimated horizontal accuracy radius in meters of this location at the 68th percentile confidence level" — [Android Location reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/location/Location). The W3C Geolocation API uses 95% instead — [gist comparing platform APIs](https://gist.github.com/shamrin/c5864ae378fe4139fbd8)
- **Independent check [measured, abstract-level]:** On high-sensitivity A-GPS phones, "estimated positional accuracy... correlated poorly with observed error" — [Zandbergen & Barbeau 2011](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/positional-accuracy-of-assisted-gps-data-from-highsensitivity-gpsenabled-mobile-phones/E1EE20CD1A301C537BEE8EC66766B0A9)
- **Device dependence [unverified]:** Accuracy information differed significantly among iPhone, iPod and iPad devices, and there is no unified certification process (HTC One S and iPhone 5s study) — [ResearchGate 275721594](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275721594_Horizontal_Accuracy_Assessment_of_Global_Positioning_System_Data_from_Common_Smartphones)
- **Threshold modelling [model, not field coverage]:** Smartphone radial error modelled as Rayleigh, with 95th percentile about 12 m. Using a 15 m accuracy gate gave 4.15% false acceptance and 3.70% false rejection — [ResearchGate 334548843](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334548843_Smartphone_GPS_accuracy_study_in_an_urban_environment) [unverified; may be a different paper than the PLOS one]
- Merry & Bettinger 2019 did **not** compare reported accuracy with actual error — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0219890)

### Inferences
- **[inference] LifePath's synthetic convention (hAcc = 1.5 × per-axis σ) is close to Apple's definition.** For circular error, DRMS = √2·σ ≈ 1.41σ, so 1.5σ is a slightly pessimistic, safe choice. It implies about 67.5% of synthetic fixes fall within hAcc (Rayleigh: 1 − exp(−1.5²/2)). This is fine, but it bakes in perfect calibration. A more realistic test makes the generator's reported hAcc only *loosely* follow the true error: right on average in open sky, too small during NLOS episodes (the dangerous case), and lagging in time.
- **[inference] Calibration can be checked on the owner's own data without ground truth only indirectly.** Use residuals to the matched OSM way (section 3) normalised by hAcc. If more than ~37% of moving fixes have perpendicular offset > hAcc on clearly matched straight streets, hAcc is over-confident there. Street centreline versus sidewalk offset (a few metres) biases this test, so apply it only where hAcc ≫ the sidewalk offset, or subtract the expected offset.
- **[inference] "94% of moving points have hAcc ≤ 20 m" says nothing about error, only about claimed error.** At 1.73× for 95%, an hAcc of 20 m means a 95% radius of about 35 m.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of what fraction of **iPhone** fixes fall within stated horizontalAccuracy (any model, any year). This would be a novel measurement; an external logger would make it possible (section 4).
- Unknown how iOS computes hAcc when fused with Wi-Fi or motion, and whether it is inflated during NLOS.

## 3. Ground-truth-free accuracy metrics

### Takeaway
The literature uses **distance-to-matched-route**, **length ratio (raw versus matched)**, **DTW/Fréchet distance between trace and matched route**, and **error autocorrelation** as reference-free quality measures. One study reports that a combined reference-free indicator agreed with ground-truth measures on smartphone active-travel data. Repeated-traversal consistency and stop scatter are standard ideas in practice, but I found no paper that uses them as a formal accuracy metric for phone trackers.

### Cited findings
- **Distance metrics [method]:** Without ground truth, authors use distance between the GPS trajectory and the matched route, and the ratio of raw trajectory length to matched route length. Caveat: "all evaluation metrics without ground truth will have some limitations" — [Rappos et al., force-directed offline map matching, arXiv 1903.12400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.12400)
- **Validated indicator [measured]:** On smartphone-based walking and cycling data, an error indicator built from reference-free measures (length index, average distance error per record, DTW, Fréchet distance, alignment) "is aligned with the ground-truth based measures" — [Berjisian et al. 2023, IET Intelligent Transport Systems](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1049/itr2.12250)
- **Flagging bad matches [method]:** One method flags matches for visual inspection: wrong-way travel, missing links in the network, parallel facilities on the same street (sidewalk versus road) — [pgMapMatch, poor-quality urban GPS](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326470236_Map-Matching_Poor-Quality_GPS_Data_in_Urban_Environments_The_pgMapMatch_Package); unsupervised identification of map-matching errors from trace plus route only — [Map-Matching Error Identification in the Absence of Ground Truth](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364813738_Map-Matching_Error_Identification_in_the_Absence_of_Ground_Truth)
- **Autocorrelation as quality [method]:** The autocorrelation C of measurement error is proposed as a quality measure for GPS movement data. The upward bias in path length is a function of C — [Ranacher et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04504)
- **Static scatter [method]:** Repeated static fixes at known points (20 visits per monument) were used to compute per-point RMSE and confidence intervals — [Merry & Bettinger 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0219890). The same idea without a known point is the spread of fixes around their own centroid during a stay, which measures precision but not bias.

### Inferences (LifePath-specific metric set; all unmeasured proposals)
- **[inference] M1, perpendicular offset to matched way:** For each moving fix on a confidently matched walking stretch, the signed perpendicular distance to the OSM way. Report median |d|, p95 and the signed mean per way. Lower bound caveat: OSM centreline versus sidewalk is a systematic offset of a few metres per side, so look at the **spread** (IQR) and at **sign flips**, not just the median. Compare raw, live filter and RTS outputs on the same stretches: the smoother should cut the spread without growing the offset, and a growing offset means corner cutting.
- **[inference] M2, path length ratio:** Length of raw, live or smoothed path divided by matched route length. Ratios well above 1 mean zig-zag noise; below 1 means corner cutting or over-smoothing. Cheap, scale-free and directly tied to Ranacher's distance bias.
- **[inference] M3, repeat-route consistency:** For the commute and shop routes, resample each traversal by arc length along the matched route, then compute per-station cross-track spread across traversals. Systematic spread that is stable across days means local multipath bias (a building); random spread means noise. Pools of 10+ traversals give a per-location error map without ground truth. This measures precision plus day-to-day bias variation; a constant bias shared by all traversals is invisible to it.
- **[inference] M4, stop scatter:** During stationary visits, the radius containing 68% and 95% of raw fixes around the stay centroid, compared with median hAcc in the same window. This is a direct calibration check of hAcc for precision. Indoors this is pessimistic, since Wi-Fi and cell fixes take over.
- **[inference] M5, self-crossings and turn-angle spectrum:** The number of self-intersections per km and the share of heading changes > 90° between consecutive points on straight matched segments. Zero is ideal on a straight street.
- **[inference] M6, filter innovation statistics:** Normalised innovation squared (NIS) of the live filter against hAcc. A mean far above 2 (for 2-D) means hAcc or the process noise is over-confident. This runs on device data with no ground truth.
- **[inference] All six can be computed on the phone or from the existing health report.** They produce summary numbers (no coordinates), so they can be shared in a public repo or issue without privacy risk.

### Gaps
- No published paper found using repeat-traversal consistency or stop scatter as a formal accuracy benchmark for a consumer tracking app.
- No published typical OSM sidewalk/centreline offset for walking-matched data; it depends on local mapping (sidewalks mapped as separate ways or not).

## 4. Cheap ground truth options without survey gear

### Takeaway
Best value: (a) **public datasets** with RTK/INS ground truth. GSDC (Android, cars, California) and UrbanNav (Hong Kong, car-mounted Xiaomi Mi 8) both exist, but **neither contains iPhone data or walking data**. (b) A **u-blox ZED-F9P** dual-frequency receiver (chip under 200 USD; boards with an antenna typically cost more), worn next to the phone, gives sub-decimetre to decimetre reference after RTKLIB processing, which runs on Windows. (c) Free "known path" ground truth from walking along well-mapped features, with metre-level uncertainty. ADVIO is the one public iPhone dataset with independent ground truth, but it is a visual-inertial odometry set, not a GNSS walking benchmark.

### Cited findings
- **GSDC 2021 [spec]:** 73 training traces with high-accuracy ground truth (NovAtel SPAN) plus 48 test traces without. Android phones in cars — [Google, GSDC 2021 description](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/Google_Smartphone_Decimeter_Challenge_2021.pdf); [ION competition page](https://www.ion.org/gnss/googlecompetition.cfm)
- **GSDC 2023–24 [spec]:** 196 drive tests, ground truth from NovAtel SPAN ISA-100C. "In-phone GPS chipset locations will not be provided", because they come from a proprietary per-model algorithm — [Kaggle GSDC 2023-2024 data page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/smartphone-decimeter-2023/data); [ION summary PDF](https://www.ion.org/gnss/upload/Smartphone-Decimeter-Challenge-2023-2024.pdf)
- **UrbanNav (Hong Kong) [spec]:** The phone is a **Xiaomi Mi 8** (raw multi-frequency GNSS at 1 Hz in RINEX 3.02; no iPhone). All data was recorded from a car roof (no pedestrian set in the NAVI paper). Ground truth is NovAtel SPAN-CPT RTK/INS post-processed in Inertial Explorer: centimetre-level when RTK is fixed, but "not guaranteed in urban canyons". Environments are medium, deep and harsh urban plus a 1.7 km tunnel. Skymasks are included. The paper reports that even FGO RMSE "can still reach more than 10 m". Data is on GitHub (IPNL-POLYU/UrbanNavDataset); the dataset license is not stated in the paper — [Hsu et al., NAVI 70(4), navi.602](https://navi.ion.org/content/70/4/navi.602)
- **ADVIO [spec]:** iPhone 6s sensor data with 6-DoF ground-truth pose from a reference rig. CoreLocation appears as an input, not as the reference — [Cortés et al., ECCV 2018](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2018/papers_ECCV/papers/Santiago_Cortes_ADVIO_An_Authentic_ECCV_2018_paper.pdf)
- **Other iPhone walking data:** AI Guide Dog (iPhone 13, 57 h of walking) uses Google Maps API GPS, not an independent reference — [arXiv 2501.07957](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.07957). A San Francisco urban-canyon study used iPhone 13 CoreLocation; I could not confirm a public release — [arXiv 2601.22406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22406)
- **Curated list:** [barbeau/awesome-gnss](https://github.com/barbeau/awesome-gnss/blob/main/README.md) indexes GNSS datasets and tools.
- **ZED-F9P price and quality [measured]:** "Typically comes at a price of less than 200 USD" (chipset) — [Hohensinn et al. 2022, Remote Sensing](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/142091/1/Hohensinn-2022-Remote%20Sensing-vor.pdf). Static post-processing found the ZED-F9P at sub-decimetre median horizontal error even under canopy; a Pixel 5 was below 0.5 m in open sky and about 1.5 m under canopy — [MDPI Sensors 23(6):3136](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/6/3136). An evaluation-kit F9P reached about 8 mm average static accuracy against a geodetic reference — [ISPRS Archives XLII-2/W17](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W17/309/2019/isprs-archives-XLII-2-W17-309-2019.pdf); PPP-RTK capabilities — [PMC10346409](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10346409/)
- **Phone PPK [measured]:** RTKLIB PPK on Android raw measurements can reach sub-metre accuracy in principle, but phone measurement quality "makes this performance difficult to achieve consistently" — [Everett et al. 2022, Optimizing RTKLIB for smartphones](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9144685/)

### Inferences
- **[inference] Public datasets are useful for LifePath only as a filter benchmark, not as an iOS benchmark.** Their phone chipset fixes are withheld (GSDC 2023), or the data is raw GNSS that must first be turned into positions (WLS via RTKLIB or GraphGNSSLib). You could compute WLS positions plus a covariance-derived "hAcc" from raw data, feed them through the LifePath filter in CI as position-only input, and score against the SPAN truth. Caveats: car dynamics, not walking; Android raw WLS is much noisier than CoreLocation's fused output; and dataset licences must allow redistribution before anything goes into the public repo. Downloading at CI time instead of committing avoids the licence question. I have not verified GSDC licence terms.
- **[inference] Cheapest real ground truth for the owner: ZED-F9P plus a multi-band antenna, logged by a phone or small laptop, post-processed (PPK) with RTKLIB on Windows against a free national or EUREF CORS base station.** Chip under $200 per the source; a complete board plus antenna is likely €200–300 (my estimate, not sourced). This would give a decimetre-level reference for walks, and so the first-ever measurement of iPhone 15 Pro error and hAcc calibration on the owner's own routes. It needs a non-trivial one-time setup, which conflicts with the "non-programmer" constraint unless scripted.
- **[inference] €0 "known path" truth:** walk a route deliberately on one side of streets whose sidewalks are mapped as separate OSM ways, or along straight features visible in aerial imagery (canal edges, park paths). The reference error is about 1–3 m (OSM and imagery alignment), so this can validate errors of about 5 m or more and the corner-cut bar (> 10 m), not sub-metre claims. Stand still for 2 min at a recognisable point (a corner or crossing) to get static samples.
- **[inference] A cheaper but weaker external reference:** an old dual-frequency Android phone running GnssLogger at the same time. Same error class as the iPhone, so it only shows *relative* disagreement. Not ground truth.

### Gaps
- No public dataset found with **iPhone CoreLocation positions plus RTK ground truth while walking**.
- Exact current retail price of complete ZED-F9P boards and antennas in the EU not checked.
- GSDC and UrbanNav redistribution licences not verified.

## 5. Regression testing with recorded real traces, and privacy in a public repo

### Takeaway
No tracker-specific "best practice" source was found. The privacy literature is clear: removing identifiers is not enough for GPS trajectories (home and work re-identification), and no single geomasking method is "approved". Translation or rotation masks keep geometry (good for filter tests) but are reversible if the base map leaks, and they break OSM map matching. So the safe pattern is real traces **kept private (not in git)** for local or on-phone replay, plus **synthetic traces** built on public OSM geometry for CI.

### Cited findings
- **Geomasking basics:** Geomasking alters individual locations before release. Translation, scaling and rotation are deterministic affine masks that preserve some spatial properties. Aggregation into grids is the simplest method — [Lin, geomasking notebook (UCGIS/I-GUIDE)](https://nbviewer.cgwebhost.cigi.illinois.edu/github/linyuehzzz/ucgis-iguide-geoprivacy/blob/main/geomasking.ipynb)
- **Trajectory re-identification risk:** Detailed GPS logs "can be used as a strong personal identifier". Removing identifiers alone does not remove the risk — [Privacy risk in GeoData: a survey, arXiv 2402.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03612v2). Home addresses can be re-identified even from Gaussian-skew-anonymised points (Cassa et al. 2008, cited in the same survey).
- **No standard:** "There is no generally recommended nor approved geomasking technique." Risk should be judged across all daily activity locations, not just home (DAL k-anonymity) — [PMC7059321](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7059321); trajectory geomasking — [Seidl, Revista Cartográfica 2017](https://doaj.org/article/ae32e3aab0aa43b1855ee54060541162); trajectory synthesis as an alternative — [CATS, arXiv 2309.11587](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.11587v1.pdf)
- **Distance bias depends on error autocorrelation**, so replay tests on real traces catch failures that white synthetic noise hides — [Ranacher et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04504)

### Inferences (proposed practice, unmeasured)
- **[inference] Golden-file pattern for CI (public repo):**
  - Synthetic ground-truth paths are generated from **public OSM geometry** of an arbitrary neutral city (or simple parametric shapes: L-turns, U-turns, stops, a tunnel gap), never from the owner's routes.
  - The correlated error model from section 1 is applied with a **fixed RNG seed** per scenario.
  - Assert on **metric thresholds** (median, p95, max corner cut, M2 length ratio, % fixes inside hAcc), not on exact coordinates, so small harmless filter changes do not churn the golden files.
  - Store the metric outputs as a small JSON "golden" for diffing between commits.
  - Run several seeds per scenario: a single seed can pass by luck. Heavy-tailed noise needs more seeds (e.g. 20) to make p95 stable.
- **[inference] Real-trace replay, kept private:** export a trace from the phone, replay it in a local or on-device test harness that runs the same Swift filter, and write **only aggregate metrics** (M1–M6) back to the repo or an issue. A private repo or GitHub encrypted artifacts would also work but add complexity.
- **[inference] If a real trace must enter the public repo:** take only a short segment (minutes) far from home and work, strip absolute timestamps (keep relative seconds), rotate and translate it to a different, random place, and drop the start and end of each segment. That is acceptable for pure filter tests, but it loses OSM matching. A better route is to "transplant" only the **error series**: estimate the error (raw minus matched or smoothed path) from a private trace, then add that error sequence to a synthetic path in a neutral city. This keeps realistic temporal correlation and NLOS shape and contains no location.
- **[inference] The "error transplant" also gives a data-driven way to fit the GM τ and σ for the synthetic generator:** compute the autocorrelation of the cross-track residual on matched straight stretches. Only aggregate parameters (τ, σ, episode rate) get committed.
